## Verdict, in words

**Confirmed.** The zero-rollover abstention hands the bots a large, load-bearing slice of
score. A bot emitting `rollovers: 0` keeps roughly 0.19–0.24 of score that a non-abstaining
treatment removes: `gaussianBot` drops a median 0.2395 (0.7186 → 0.4791) and `replayBot` drops
0.2131 (0.7671 → 0.5540). Under the counterfactual, **34/50 gaussianBot seeds and 49/50
replayBot seeds cross back below the 0.70 human threshold** — the two generators that this
repo's earlier sweep
(`research/experiments/2026-08-02-bot-fixtures-vs-human-threshold/`) showed clearing the
threshold are the two the abstention was carrying over it. The drop far exceeds the 0.03
refutation bar in the hypothesis, so the abstention is doing the work, not a rounding effect.

The human median is untouched: `medDrop=0.0000`, `medActiveWeight=1.00`, 0/50 crossing back.
The human fixture emits rollovers on essentially every seed, so its `rolloverRate` never
abstains and the counterfactual changes nothing for it. The gap is exactly what the mechanism
predicts (`src/analyzer.ts:241` returns `NO_DATA` at `rollovers === 0`;
`src/analyzer.ts:288-295` drops a `NO_DATA` metric from both numerator and denominator): the
highest-weighted metric, 0.25, silently stops voting on precisely the inputs it would score
lowest.

**The cross-reference that stops this being a patch.** The naive fix — force zero rollovers to
vote 0, which is what this counterfactual measures — has a measured real-human cost.
`research/experiments/2026-08-02-zero-rollover-abstention-candidates/` runs that exact rule
(candidate A) over the CMU corpus: `cmu_A` moves **14 real-human windows below the
non-abstaining floor** and drops `fracAtOrAbove0.70` from 0.833 to 0.586, because real humans
typing short deliberate passages legitimately produce zero rollovers (646 of 2448 CMU windows
abstain on rollover). So the hole is real and large, and closing it the obvious way charges
real humans for it. Candidate B in that experiment is the softer alternative; picking between
them is a product judgment for `api-steward`, and any code change routes through
`/cadence:build`.

## Evidence-ladder rung

**In-repo fixtures for the bots, cross-referenced to the real CMU corpus for the human cost.**
The bot side is the bottom rung — `gaussianBot`/`replayBot`/`constantBot`/`randomJitterBot`
are `tests/fixtures/bot-profiles.ts`, generated by this repo. The size of the free score
(0.19–0.24, 34–49/50 crossing back) is a fact about those fixtures against the 0.70 threshold,
not about real automation. The human-cost half is one rung higher: the 14-windows figure it
leans on comes from the CMU benchmark
(`research/experiments/2026-08-02-zero-rollover-abstention-candidates/`,
`research/papers/killourhy-maxion-2009-ks-benchmark.md`), real captured keystrokes. The
"free score" claim and the "naive fix hurts humans" claim therefore stand on different rungs;
both are stated at the rung their data reaches.

## What this does not show

- Nothing about real bots. The free score is measured on synthetic bot fixtures. A real
  automation framework may or may not emit `rollovers: 0` — that is a browser-behaviour
  question for `browser-expert` and, if it wants a number, a live-capture experiment.
- The counterfactual "zero rollovers score 0" is a design choice this experiment adopts to
  size the gap, not a proven-correct rule. Its human cost is exactly why the candidates
  experiment exists; this file measures the size of the hole, not whether it is safe to close.
- Nothing about the full pipeline: `analyze` was fed `TimingData` directly. "crossBack < 0.70"
  counts raw scores dropping below the threshold, not live `classification` transitions, which
  also depend on state history (`src/index.ts:12-27`).
- `medActiveWeight` for `replayBot` (0.65) is above the bots' 0.50 because a replayed human's
  corrections/bursts sometimes clear their own abstention gates; the reconstruction of `S` is
  printed per-run so it stays inspectable rather than assumed.
